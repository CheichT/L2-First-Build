A frog, driven by hunger, made his way to the edge of the lake in search of flies. He slipped beneath the surface and settled into stillness, waiting with the quiet discipline of a creature who understood patience. 
For a long while, nothing stirred. Then, at last, a shimmer above the water caught his eye — what appeared to be a golden cloud of flies drifting lazily in the afternoon light. 
He looked again, only to find it was a crumpled piece of paper carried on the breeze. 
He lunged for it regardless, unwilling to concede the effort. But when he opened his mouth, he discovered only a note, weathered and faint, that read: "Gone fishing." 
So consumed was he by the indignity of it that he never heard the slow, deliberate movement beneath the reeds — until a snake, who had observed the whole affair with cool patience, brought the matter to its final end, and the frog was never seen again.
